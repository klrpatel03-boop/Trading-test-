# Knowing It's Working: A Guide to Tracking My Body Work

*Active + passive conditioning — goals, calls to action, and the feedback that
tells me it's actually happening.*

---

## Start here: the real problem this solves

I'm not bad at doing the work. I plan, and I execute. What I've been missing is a
way to **know how it's going** — especially the slow, invisible stuff. Tendons
remodel over 12+ weeks. Fascia and recovery adaptations don't give me a daily
soreness signal. So I do the work, but I can't *feel* it landing, and that
uncertainty is the gap.

**The fix is not more planning. It's instrumentation — a feedback loop.**

The whole guide rests on one distinction:

- **Leading indicators = the behaviors I control.** Did I train? Did I do my tendon dose? Did I sleep 7+ hours? Did I do my breathing? These I can check **today**, and they're either green or red. Instant feedback.
- **Lagging indicators = the outcomes I want.** Strength, jump power, tendon stiffness, mobility, resilience. These move **slowly** — I only see them on a scheduled **retest**, not day to day.

> **The core promise: if the leading indicators stay green, the lagging ones
> WILL follow.** My job day-to-day is to keep the behaviors green and *trust the
> process*. My job every few weeks is to retest and confirm. That's it. That's
> how I'll always know it's happening — even when I can't feel it.

---

## The two halves of the work

**Active conditioning** — what I *do*. The kettlebell program: swings, snatches,
get-ups, presses, windmills, carries. Power, strength, rotational output.

**Passive conditioning** — what I *restore and build quietly*. Recovery
stretching, tendon isometric "doses," mobility, sleep, hydration, collagen
timing. This is the half with no instant feedback — so it's the half that needs
tracking the most.

Both are conditioning. One is loud, one is silent. The silent one is where most
people quit, because they can't tell it's working. **The tracking is what keeps
me in it.**

## Goals → Call to Action → How I'll Know

This is the heart of the guide. Each goal gets a concrete action **and** a signal
that proves it's moving.

### Goal 1 — Functional & explosive power
- **Call to action:** Ballistic day weekly — swings and snatches at max intent, full rest ("power loves rest"). Protect bar speed; never let it become a grind.
- **Leading (this week):** Did I hit my ballistic session? Did every rep feel *crisp*, not grinding?
- **Lagging (retest every ~4 weeks):** Broad jump distance or vertical jump. **If power is building, the jump improves.**

### Goal 2 — Maximal & unilateral strength
- **Call to action:** Grind day weekly — get-up, one-arm press, front squat, single-leg RDL at HSR tempo (3s/3s), riding the 15RM→6RM feel across 12 weeks.
- **Leading:** Did I complete my grind sets at the prescribed tempo without form breakdown?
- **Lagging:** Hardest tempo/pause I can hold on the press or clean; reps at a given feel. **More quality reps or slower tempo at the same load = stronger.**

### Goal 3 — Rotational power & the oblique slings
- **Call to action:** Rotational day weekly — windmill, bent press (or hold), rotational swing, renegade row, suitcase carry.
- **Leading:** Did I do my rotational session? Did I keep the lumbar braced and rotate from hips/thoracic?
- **Lagging:** Windmill depth/range; carry distance at load; **left–right symmetry** (see below).

### Goal 4 — Tendon stiffness & resilience *(the invisible one)*
- **Call to action:** 2× weekly tendon dose — 4×30s isometric holds (Baar) or 4×6s ~90% holds (Arampatzis). Hold the protocol **constant for 12 weeks**. Keep it short (<10 min) and pain-free; re-load after 6–8h if doubling.
- **Leading:** Did I do my two doses this week? (This is nearly the *whole* game — consistency over novelty.)
- **Lagging:** Joint comfort under load; ability to handle heavier/faster work without niggles; RFD proxies (jump). **Tendons are a 12-week story — judge them on the Week 13 retest, not this week.**

### Goal 5 — Fascial / elastic springiness
- **Call to action:** Elastic work on ballistic day — hand-to-hand swings, flows, springy oscillatory movement with a pre-stretch.
- **Leading:** Did I include elastic/flow work? Did it feel *bouncy and relaxed*, not muscled?
- **Lagging:** Smoothness and "float" in swings/snatches; how effortless the catapult feels over weeks.

### Goal 6 — Mobility & recovery quality *(passive)*
- **Call to action:** Block C (full recovery flow) on rest days; Block D (breathing + wind-down) nightly; reweighted toward **grip/wrist, hips, posterior chain, shoulders, obliques**.
- **Leading:** Did I do Block D tonight? Did I do Block C on my rest day? Wrist/forearm work daily?
- **Lagging:** Morning stiffness (should drop); range of motion in key stretches; how fast I feel recovered between sessions.

### Goal 7 — Grip / wrist / forearm durability *(the #1 training-interrupter)*
- **Call to action:** Wrist flexor/extensor stretch + wrist CARs **daily**; bottoms-up work in the program; hook grip + deep handle insert + filed calluses.
- **Leading:** Did I do my daily wrist work? Any hand tears or forearm bruising this week? (Red flag = back off ballistic volume.)
- **Lagging:** Grip endurance in carries/holds; hands staying intact through heavy snatch sets.

## The instruments (how I actually see it)

I don't need a lab. I need four cheap, repeatable signals:

### 1. The daily 30-second log
The smallest possible instrument. Every day, mark the **leading indicators** green
or red. Nothing else. See `PROGRESS_LOG.md` for the ready-to-fill template. The
point isn't the data — it's that **a row of greens *is* the proof the work is
happening**, on the days I can't feel it.

### 2. The weekly check-in (5 min, same day each week)
Once a week, glance back at the row of days and ask:
- Did I hit all four session types (or three, on a 3-day week)?
- Two tendon doses done?
- Block D most nights?
- Any red flags (hand tears, joint pain, sleep < 6h repeatedly)?
- One number: average sleep, or total training sessions. Write it down.

### 3. The retest (every 4 weeks, full retest at Week 13)
This is where the **lagging** indicators finally show themselves. Same tests,
same conditions, every time:
- **Power:** broad jump or vertical (mark the distance).
- **Strength:** hardest press/clean tempo or reps at a set feel.
- **Rotational/symmetry:** windmill depth; left vs right on single-leg RDL, get-up, press.
- **Joint comfort:** any cranky tendon? (1–10 scale.)
- **Video:** film front + side; compare to last month.

**Reading the retest** (from the program's own logic):
- Power stalled → add full-recovery ballistic intent, cut density.
- Strength stalled → more unilateral/bottoms-up, slower tempo.
- Tendon cranky → tilt toward slow/isometric, away from ballistics for 2–4 weeks.

### 4. The subjective dashboard (the honest gut-check)
Numbers lag. These move first and tell me it's working *before* the retest does:
- **Morning stiffness** — trending down?
- **Grip feel** — hands and forearms handling load?
- **Sleep quality** — falling asleep faster, waking rested?
- **Energy / mood** — steadier?
- **"Do I own the movement?"** — get-ups and windmills feeling smooth, not shaky?

If these are drifting up while the numbers are flat, **the work is working** —
the retest just hasn't caught up yet.

## Reading the signals honestly

- **Green leading + flat lagging (early):** Normal. Adaptation lags behavior, especially tendon/fascia. Stay the course; the Week 13 retest is the judge.
- **Green leading + rising lagging:** It's working. Keep going, progress the levers.
- **Red leading:** *This* is the actionable one — I'm not doing the behavior. Fix the behavior, not the plan. (Usually it's a friction problem: dose too long, session too complex, no set time. Simplify.)
- **Green leading + falling lagging or rising pain:** Overreaching or a tissue that needs the slow/isometric tilt. Back off intensity, keep consistency.

The trap to avoid: **reacting to lagging indicators day to day.** They're noisy
short-term. I judge behaviors daily and outcomes monthly — never the reverse.

---

## The one-paragraph operating system

Keep the leading indicators green — train the four session types, do two tendon
doses, do Block D nightly, do my wrist work daily. Log it in 30 seconds so a row
of greens proves it's happening even when I can't feel it. Check in weekly.
Retest every four weeks and fully at Week 13. Trust that green behaviors produce
the slow outcomes, because they do. **Plan once, then let the log do the
knowing.**
