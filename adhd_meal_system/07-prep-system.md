# 07 — The "Good Day" Prep System

You said you can cook and even enjoy it on good days. **Good days are a resource — spend
them buying down future bad days.** The trick is to never *rely* on cooking, but to
capture it when the energy is there. 20–40 minutes on a good day can make a whole week
of bad days survivable.

> **Rule:** prep is always *optional* and *opportunistic*. The system fully works with
> zero prep (pouches + assembly). Prep is a force-multiplier, not a requirement. Never
> let "I didn't meal prep" become a reason the week fails.

---

## Ride the dopamine: prep when motivation strikes, not on a schedule

Classic "Sunday meal prep for 3 hours" fails ADHD — it's a big, boring, scheduled chore
that you'll dread and skip. Instead:

- **When you feel a cooking spark, use it immediately**, whatever day it is. Motivation
  is the scarce resource; capture it the moment it appears.
- **Always cook double.** Making dinner anyway? Make 2–4 portions. Leftovers are
  effortless prep you got "for free" while feeding yourself tonight.
- **Stack it with dopamine:** put on a podcast/show/music, make it the *fun* version of
  the chore. Body-double with a friend on video if that helps you start.

---

## The 3 highest-leverage things to batch

You don't need elaborate meal prep. Just having these three on hand collapses the effort
of every default meal:

1. **A protein** — batch-cook chicken, ground turkey, or a tofu tray. Portion into
   containers. This single thing turns "burrito bowl / salad / pasta / tacos" into
   30-second assembly all week.
2. **A grain** — a pot of rice/quinoa portioned out (or just keep pouches; both fine).
3. **Grab-and-go protein snacks** — hard-boil a dozen eggs, portion cottage cheese,
   pre-bag nuts/edamame. Stock the crash shelf.

That's it. Protein + grain + snacks prepped = every meal on the menu becomes assembly.

---

## "Cook once, eat 4x" anchor meals (the Sunday dinner slot)

The menu's Sunday slot is a **make-a-tray** meal that seeds the week with leftovers.
Pick one when the mood strikes; each gives 4+ portions of high-protein, high-fiber food
that reheats perfectly:

- **Big-batch chili** (turkey/beef + 3 kinds of beans + tomato) — huge fiber, freezes great.
- **Sheet-pan chicken + veg + potatoes** — one pan, minimal cleanup.
- **Curry or stew** over rice — dump-and-simmer, scales easily.
- **Egg + bean breakfast burritos** — assembly-line a dozen, wrap, freeze. *(A grab-and-
  reheat breakfast for the days when even pouring oats is too much.)*
- **A casserole / baked pasta** with high-protein pasta + lean meat + veg.

Portion into single containers the moment it's done (this is the key step — bulk in a pot
won't get eaten; single grab-able containers will).

---

## The freezer is your past self helping your future self

- Freeze single-portion leftovers labeled with what they are. Future-you on a crash day
  reheats a real meal in 3 minutes instead of hitting the drive-thru.
- Freeze burritos, soup portions, cooked protein, smoothie packs (pre-bagged frozen
  fruit + spinach → dump in blender + protein + liquid).
- A stocked freezer is **executive function in a box** — it's decisions and effort you
  already spent, banked for when you have none.

---

## The minimum viable prep (if "prep" feels like too much)

Genuinely don't have a good day this week? The floor is fine:
1. Buy the pouches, pre-cooked protein, and crash-shelf items (zero cooking).
2. Cook *one* protein, *one* time, *double* — even just brown a pound of turkey.
3. That's enough. The system was built to run on near-zero prep.

Anything beyond this is a bonus that makes the week easier — never a debt you owe.
