# 02 — The Anchor Schedule (for when every meal is hard)

This is the part that beats time-blindness. Read it once, set it up.

You told me **all three meals are hard** and you have **no reliable hunger cues**. So
this schedule doesn't lean on a "good appetite window" or on you ever *feeling* hungry.
It treats every meal as the same thing: an **alarm-driven task, anchored to a habit you
already do, with a no-effort floor underneath it.**

The schedule has two layers:
1. **Alarms** — the external trigger (your phone yells).
2. **Anchors** — attaching each meal to a routine you *already* do reliably.

Together they make the meal happen even when your time-sense and hunger are offline.

---

## The medication reality

Stimulants suppress appetite, often unpredictably across the day. The practical
consequence isn't "eat at window X" — it's simpler:

> **Hunger will not show up to remind you. For any meal, at any time, assume you won't
> feel like eating, and eat on the alarm anyway.**

Two tools make eating-without-appetite easier on *any* meal:
- **Drink it.** When solid food feels like too much (no appetite or low energy), a
  protein smoothie/shake goes down when a plate won't. This works at breakfast, lunch,
  *or* dinner — it's not a midday-only trick.
- **Small and protein-first.** A few bites of something protein-dense beats a big plate
  you'll abandon. Volume can come later in the day if appetite returns.

There's often a **late-day appetite rebound** as meds wear off. That's real hunger from
a low-intake day. Plan a satisfying dinner (or reheatable leftovers) so the rebound
lands on good food instead of takeout.

## Your daily anchor schedule

Fill in your real anchors in the right column. Examples given. Tune the times to you.

| Alarm (tune to you) | Meal | Anchor it to (a habit you already do) | Default gear |
|---------------------|------|----------------------------------------|--------------|
| ~30 min post-wake | **Breakfast** | _"After I pour my first coffee"_ | Reheat leftover / overnight oats / shake |
| ~12:30 pm | **Lunch** | _"When I step away from work"_ | **Leftovers** from last cook (or a shake) |
| ~3:30 pm | **Snack** | _"After my afternoon [walk / dog out]"_ | Grab-and-eat protein |
| ~6:30 pm | **Dinner** | _"After I close the laptop / get home"_ | **Cook** (the fun gear) or reheat |
| ~8:30 pm (optional) | **Evening snack** | _"When I sit down to wind down"_ | Protein + fiber, then kitchen closed |

> **Habit-stacking formula:** *"After I [existing habit], I [eat the meal]."*
> The existing habit is the trigger your brain already obeys; the meal rides on it.

Notice the rhythm that fits *you*: **dinner is the cook slot** (evening, you're home,
cooking is the fun engagement) and it's deliberately big so its **leftovers become the
next day's low-effort breakfast and lunch.** You only have to summon cooking energy
*once*, in the slot where it's most enjoyable.

## Alarm setup scripts (copy these labels)

Recurring phone alarms, labeled with the *action* so the alarm makes the decision for
you (not "12:30" — that makes you decide; "EAT — reheat last night's batch" *is* the
decision):

- `🍳 EAT — reheat leftovers / overnight oats / shake. No hunger needed.`
- `🥡 LUNCH — leftovers from last cook (or drink a shake). Eat anyway.`
- `🥜 Snack — grab protein. Heads off the late crash.`
- `🍳 DINNER — cook the batch (the fun part) or reheat. Make extra.`
- `🌙 Evening snack — optional, protein + fiber, then kitchen closed.`

Optional but powerful:
- A **"kitchen closed" alarm** ~1.5 hrs before bed to bookend eating and stop grazing.
- A **hydration nudge** every 2–3 hrs — dehydration mimics both hunger and fatigue, and
  stimulants are dehydrating.

---

## If your schedule is irregular (shift work, no fixed wake time)

Anchor to **events**, not the clock: meal 1 = "within ~1 hr of waking, whenever that
is"; meal 2 = "4–5 hrs later"; etc. The *spacing* matters more than the exact times.
`meal_planner.py today` doesn't care what time it is — it just tells you the meals.

---

## Why this beats a normal meal plan

A normal plan says "eat lunch at noon" and trusts you to remember, decide, and want it.
This plan says: *an alarm fires → it's stuck to a habit you already do → it names the
exact food → that food is already cooked in your fridge → and if even that's too much,
the floor is "open and eat."* Every failure point of ADHD eating has a backstop, and
none of them costs much money.
