# 09 — Trusted Recipes & Cookbooks

You wanted this to be as clear as possible to actually *do* — and the best way is to
cook from **trusted, tested recipes with exact quantities**, not vague paragraphs. So
every meal in this system links out to vetted online cookbooks. This page is the
reference; the app (`app/`) wires these links into each recipe and lets you search any
dish across all of them.

> **The rule of thumb:** Anchor's own recipe cards are the *quick version* (assembly,
> rough amounts). When you want the full, foolproof, tested version with precise
> quantities — tap through to one of these.

---

| Source | Free? | Best for | Why it's here |
|--------|-------|----------|---------------|
| **[Budget Bytes](https://www.budgetbytes.com/)** | ✅ | **Cheapest-per-serving cooking** | Your #1. *Every* recipe lists an exact cost per serving + a full cost breakdown. Tested, simple, batch/meal-prep focused. Literally built for cheap, clear cooking. |

| **[NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/)** | 💲 partial | Gold standard | The most rigorously tested recipes online with huge reader notes. Some free; full access is a subscription. |

**Start with Budget Bytes.** It's free, every recipe is priced, and it overlaps almost
perfectly with this system's cheap-and-high-protein menu.

## How to use this so it's genuinely easy

1. **In the app:** open any meal → tap **"Cook it from a tested recipe."** You get the
   exact tested page (or a search button into Budget Bytes + the others).
2. **Searching a craving:** the **Recipes** screen has one search box that builds a
   results link for every trusted site at once. Type "lentil soup," get seven options.
3. **Picking by cost:** Budget Bytes' cost-per-serving numbers + this system's
   **Cheap food index** (protein/fiber per dollar) tell you what's actually cheapest.
4. **Following along while cooking:** keep the recipe open on your phone; check off
   ingredients as you add them — the tested recipe is the precise version, Anchor's
   card is the cheat-sheet version.

> Why trusted sources matter for an ADHD kitchen: a tested recipe removes the
> mid-cook "wait, how much…?" decision that derails things. Exact quantities = less
> executive load = the meal actually happens.
