# 08 — The Budget & Cost Model (cheapest eating in Andover, MA)

Goal: eat high-protein, high-fiber, body-comp-friendly food for the **lowest monthly
cost possible**, in the Merrimack Valley. Good news: the cheapest foods on earth are
also the highest in protein and fiber, so eating cheap and eating well are the same
move. The only thing that makes healthy eating expensive is *convenience* (takeout,
pre-made, single-serve) — and convenience is exactly what the batch-cook + leftover
system replaces.

> Quick numbers: `python3 meal_planner.py budget`

---

## What this costs per month (one person)

Real, sustainable tiers cooking from staples at Market-Basket-type prices:

| Tier | $/day | $/month | What it looks like |
|------|-------|---------|--------------------|
| **Rock bottom** | ~$5–6 | **~$150–180** | Beans/lentils/eggs/oats/rice forward, chicken thighs occasionally, minimal dairy, almost no processed anything |
| **Comfortable cheap** (target) | ~$8–10 | **~$240–300** | The full menu in `03` — variety, plenty of chicken/eggs/dairy, frozen veg, fruit |
| **Relaxed** | ~$11–13 | **~$330–390** | Above + more meat, more fresh produce, some convenience items |

The system targets the **comfortable cheap** tier — genuinely low cost without feeling
like deprivation, which matters because deprivation diets fail the ADHD brain fast.

> Note: these are *grocery* costs. Andover is a high-cost town overall (rent/housing
> dominate the budget) — food is one of the few line items you can actually crush, and
> cooking from staples is the lever. This system can't lower your rent, but it can keep
> food to ~$250/month.

## Where to shop (Merrimack Valley strategy)

You're in **Market Basket country** — this is the single biggest cost lever you have.

| Store | Use it for | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Market Basket** (Tewksbury HQ; locations across the Merrimack Valley) | **Your main store for everything** | Famously low prices in the region. Great on produce, meat, staples, store brand. This is the default. |
| **Aldi** (nearby Merrimack Valley towns) | Staples, eggs, dairy, frozen veg, canned goods | Rock-bottom on store-brand basics. Quick in-and-out (small store = less ADHD wander). |
| **Costco / BJ's** (Nashua NH no-tax / Haverhill) | **Bulk:** oats, rice, eggs, frozen chicken, peanut butter, olive oil, protein powder | Only worth it for things you'll actually use up. Nashua NH = no sales tax on non-food. Skip if a membership doesn't pay for itself. |
| **Ethnic/international markets** | Dried lentils, beans, rice, spices in bulk | Often *dramatically* cheaper for legumes, rice, and spices than mainstream stores. |
| ~~Whole Foods / Star Market~~ | Avoid for staples | Same food, much higher price. |

**Tactics that cut the bill hard:**
- **Buy dried beans and lentils, not canned** — roughly 1/3 the cost per serving, and a
  pot of them is a cooking project you'll enjoy. (Canned is fine as the floor-gear
  convenience; dried is the budget default.)
- **Buy the cheap cuts of protein:** bone-in chicken thighs and whole chickens, not
  breasts. Eggs and canned tuna are the cheapest animal protein per gram, period.
- **Buy staple carbs in bulk:** big bags of oats and rice are pennies per serving.
- **Frozen veg over fresh** for cooking — cheaper, zero waste (no rotting in the
  drawer), and just as nutritious. Fresh only for what you'll eat raw soon.
- **Unit price, not sticker price.** Compare $/lb or $/oz on the shelf tag. The big bag
  almost always wins if you'll use it.
- **Shop the same cart weekly** (`04-grocery-list.md`) — online pickup/reorder removes
  the in-store ADHD wander that fills the cart with impulse buys.

## The budget rules

1. **Cook from staples; pay the convenience tax only on the floor (depleted days).**
   Dried beans most days; a can of tuna when you're fried. Both are cheap; the dried
   version is cheaper.
2. **Batch-cook → leftovers are the cheapest food you own.** Cooking once for 4–6
   servings beats buying 4–6 separate convenience meals on every axis: money, effort,
   nutrition.
3. **Buy the cheap protein cuts and buy carbs in bulk.** Thighs not breasts, eggs and
   legumes always, big bags of oats/rice.
4. **Frozen veg over fresh for cooking** — cheaper and zero waste.
5. **One store run a week, same cart, never hungry/unmedicated** — fewer impulse buys.
6. **Track waste, not pennies.** The biggest budget leak for an ADHD kitchen isn't price
   per item — it's **food that rots because you forgot it existed** (object
   permanence). Frozen veg, freezing leftovers, and a "eat-me-first" fridge shelf fix
   more of your budget than coupon-clipping ever will.
