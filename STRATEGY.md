## IRA Trading Rules and Constraints

Understanding IRA-specific rules is critical before placing any trades.

### What You CAN Do

- Buy and sell stocks, ETFs, mutual funds, and bonds
- Trade as frequently as you want (no pattern day trader rule concerns since no margin)
- Rebalance positions **without triggering taxable events**
- Reinvest dividends automatically (DRIP)
- Buy fractional shares at Fidelity

### What You CANNOT Do

- **No margin trading** -- IRAs are cash accounts only
- **No short selling** -- you cannot borrow shares to sell
- **No selling naked options** -- only covered calls and cash-secured puts are permitted
- **No contribution above IRS limits** -- $7,000/year for 2025-2026 (under 50)
- **Settlement rules apply** -- trades settle T+1 for stocks/ETFs; you must wait for settlement before reusing funds (free-riding violation otherwise)

### Tax Advantages

- **Tax-deferred growth**: No capital gains taxes on trades within the IRA
- **No tax on dividends**: Dividends reinvested without tax drag
- **No tax on rebalancing**: Sell winners and buy laggards freely
- **Tax due only at withdrawal**: Distributions taxed as ordinary income in retirement

## Entry Strategy: Lump Sum vs Dollar-Cost Averaging

### Recommendation: Lump Sum

For a $1,200 account, **lump sum investing is the clear winner**:

1. **Statistical edge**: Lump sum beats DCA roughly 2/3 of the time historically (Vanguard study). Markets trend up over time, so being invested sooner captures more upside.
2. **Minimal absolute risk**: The maximum downside on $1,200 is small in dollar terms. Even a severe 30% drawdown is a $360 loss -- painful but recoverable and far less consequential than on a $120,000 portfolio.
3. **Transaction simplicity**: At Fidelity with $0 commissions and fractional shares, you can deploy the full $1,200 in one trading session.
4. **Opportunity cost**: Sitting in cash with $1,200 while DCA-ing $100/month over a year means 11 months of reduced market exposure. Time in the market matters more than timing the market at this scale.

### Execution Plan

1. Fund the IRA with the $1,200 rollover
2. Place all core ETF orders in a single session (use fractional shares)
3. Deploy satellite positions on the same day or within the same week
4. Keep 5% ($60) as a cash reserve for opportunistic buys on pullbacks

## Rebalancing Rules

### Quarterly Rebalancing (Core)

- Review core allocations every quarter (January, April, July, October)
- Rebalance if any core position drifts more than **5 percentage points** from target
- Use new contributions to rebalance first (avoid selling when possible)
- In an IRA, selling to rebalance has zero tax impact -- use this advantage

### Monthly Review (Satellite)

- Assess satellite thesis validity monthly
- Rotate out of positions where the thesis has broken down
- Replace with new high-conviction ideas from the watchlist

### Contribution-Based Rebalancing

- When adding new money (up to $7,000/year), direct it to the most underweight position
- This naturally rebalances without any selling

## Risk Management Rules

### Position Sizing

- No single stock > 5% of portfolio ($60 max for individual names)
- No single sector ETF > 10% of portfolio ($120 max)
- Core index ETFs can be up to 35% individually (they are already diversified)

### Drawdown Rules

- **-10% portfolio drawdown**: Review all satellite positions; tighten stops
- **-15% individual satellite position**: Mandatory stop-loss; sell and reassess
- **-20% portfolio drawdown**: Shift to 90% core / 10% satellite; reduce risk

### What NOT to Do

- Do not panic sell during broad market corrections (core positions are long-term)
- Do not chase meme stocks or speculative assets in a retirement account
- Do not over-trade -- even though it is commission-free, excessive trading leads to poor timing decisions
- Do not concentrate in a single stock or sector beyond the limits above

## Why This Works in a Tax-Advantaged IRA

1. **Free rebalancing**: In a taxable account, selling winners to rebalance triggers capital gains tax. In an IRA, you can rebalance as often as needed with zero tax friction.

2. **Dividend compounding**: SCHD and other dividend payers distribute income that would be taxable in a regular account. In an IRA, all dividends compound tax-free.

3. **Tactical flexibility**: You can rotate satellite positions without worrying about short-term vs long-term capital gains rates. This is a major advantage for active management of the satellite sleeve.

4. **Tax-inefficient assets belong here**: REITs, high-yield bonds, and actively managed funds (if used) are most tax-efficient inside an IRA. This gives you freedom to explore these asset classes in the satellite portion.

5. **Long time horizon**: Retirement accounts are inherently long-term. This aligns perfectly with a core-satellite approach where the core compounds for decades and the satellite adds incremental alpha over time.
