# The Smart Recovery & Mobility Routine

A full-body, whole-person recovery system designed to keep every major muscle
group healthy, calm the nervous system, and maximize how well you bounce back
between training days. It blends the best ideas from leading mobility and
recovery teachers (see [Methodology & Sources](#methodology--credits)).

> **The core principle:** Recovery is *active*, not passive. The goal isn't just
> to "feel loose" — it's to down-regulate your nervous system, restore range of
> motion, improve blood flow, and reset both body **and** mind.

---

## How to Use This Routine

Pick the block that fits your day. Every block is built from the same exercise
library below, so you can mix and match.

| When | Block | Time | Goal |
|------|-------|------|------|
| Before training | **A — Dynamic Prep** | 8–12 min | Wake up muscles, raise temperature, mobilize joints |
| After training | **B — Cooldown Flush** | 10–15 min | Bring heart rate down, gentle static stretch, breathe |
| Rest / off day | **C — Full Recovery Flow** | 25–35 min | Deep mobility, full body, nervous-system reset |
| Before bed | **D — Wind-Down** | 8–10 min | Parasympathetic ("rest & digest") activation, sleep prep |

**Golden rules**
1. **Never stretch cold muscle hard.** Do 2–3 min of light movement first (march in place, arm circles, easy bike).
2. **Dynamic before training, static after.** Static stretching before lifting/sprinting can temporarily reduce power; save the long holds for after or rest days.
3. **Breathe into the stretch.** Long exhales (out longer than in) tell your nervous system it's safe to relax and let you gain range.
4. **Stretch to mild tension (4–6/10), never sharp pain.** Pain triggers a protective reflex that tightens the very muscle you're trying to open.
5. **Consistency > intensity.** 10 honest minutes daily beats one brutal hour a week.

## Block A — Dynamic Prep (Pre-Training, 8–12 min)

Movement-based. Each for 8–12 reps or ~30 sec per side. Goal: temperature up, joints lubricated, tissues elastic.

1. **World's Greatest Stretch** — 5/side. Lunge, elbow to instep, rotate, reach up. Opens hips, T-spine, hamstrings, groin all at once.
2. **Leg swings** — front/back ×10, side/side ×10 each leg. Hips and hamstrings.
3. **Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)** — 3 slow circles each direction, each hip. (Joint health — see FRC below.)
4. **Walking spiderman + reach** — 6/side. Hip flexors, adductors, thoracic rotation.
5. **Cat–Cow** — ×8. Spinal segmentation.
6. **Arm circles + band pull-aparts** — 15 each. Shoulders, upper back.
7. **Ankle rocks** — 10/side. Knee-over-toe to mobilize ankles (huge for squats/running).
8. **Bodyweight squats to depth** — ×10, slow. Greases the full lower-body pattern.

---

## Block B — Cooldown Flush (Post-Training, 10–15 min)

Gentle static holds, 30–45 sec each. Heart rate should be dropping. This is where you nudge range of motion back.

1. **Standing forward fold** — hamstrings, lower back. Let head hang.
2. **Couch stretch / kneeling hip flexor** — 45 sec/side. The #1 stretch for anyone who sits.
3. **Figure-4 glute stretch** — lying or seated, 45 sec/side.
4. **Pigeon pose** — 60 sec/side. Deep glute + hip rotators.
5. **Chest/pec doorway stretch** — 45 sec/side. Reverses rounded posture.
6. **Cross-body & overhead triceps** — 30 sec/side. Shoulders.
7. **Standing quad stretch** — 30 sec/side.
8. **Child's pose** — 60 sec. Lats, spine, breathing reset.

---

## Block C — Full Recovery Flow (Rest Day, 25–35 min)

The complete head-to-toe session. Move slowly, breathe, hold static stretches **60–120 sec** (longer holds on rest days help tissue remodeling and parasympathetic tone). Go top to bottom so you don't miss anything.

### 1. Neck & Jaw (often ignored — huge for tension headaches)
- **Neck CARs** — slow circles, 3 each way.
- **Upper trap stretch** — ear to shoulder, hand gently assists, 45 sec/side.
- **Levator scapulae** — look toward armpit, 45 sec/side.
- **Jaw release** — open/close slow ×10, then hold mouth gently open 20 sec. Carries a surprising amount of stress.

### 2. Shoulders, Chest & Upper Back
- **Thread the needle** — 60 sec/side. T-spine rotation.
- **Pec doorway stretch** at 3 heights (low/mid/high) — 30 sec each.
- **Lat stretch** (kneel, arms on bench/couch, sink chest) — 60 sec.
- **Sleeper stretch** (internal rotation) — 45 sec/side. Key for overhead athletes.

### 3. Spine & Core
- **Cat–Cow** — ×10.
- **Cobra / sphinx** — 60 sec. Extends a flexion-dominant spine.
- **Supine spinal twist** — 60 sec/side. Decompresses lower back.
- **Seated forward fold** — 90 sec. Whole posterior chain.

### 4. Hips & Glutes (the recovery epicenter for most people)
- **90/90 hip switches** — 8 slow reps, then hold each side 45 sec. Best all-around hip rotation drill.
- **Pigeon** — 90 sec/side.
- **Frog stretch** — 90 sec. Adductors/groin.
- **Couch stretch** — 90 sec/side. Hip flexors + quads.

### 5. Hamstrings & Posterior Chain
- **Single-leg supine hamstring** (strap optional) — 60 sec/side.
- **Standing wide-leg fold** — 90 sec. Hamstrings + adductors.

### 6. Quads & Knees
- **Standing or side-lying quad** — 60 sec/side.
- **Kneeling quad (on a pad)** — 45 sec/side.

### 7. Calves, Ankles & Feet
- **Downward dog calf pedal** — 60 sec.
- **Wall calf stretch** (straight + bent knee for gastroc & soleus) — 45 sec each/side.
- **Toe & plantar fascia stretch** (toes up on wall) — 45 sec/side.

### 8. Wrists & Forearms (don't skip — grip/desk recovery)
- **Wrist flexor/extensor stretch** — 30 sec each direction/side.

## Block D — Wind-Down for Sleep (8–10 min)

Done in bed or on the floor with dim lights. Everything here is about flipping the nervous system into "rest & digest." Hold each 60–90 sec, eyes closed, breathing slow.

1. **Box / 4-7-8 breathing** — 2 min. (Inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8.) Single best lever for sleep.
2. **Legs up the wall** — 2–3 min. Drains tired legs, lowers heart rate.
3. **Supine twist** — 60 sec/side.
4. **Reclined butterfly** — 90 sec. Opens hips passively.
5. **Happy baby** — 60 sec. Releases lower back.
6. **Child's pose → final stillness** — slow nasal breathing, longer exhales.

## The "Smart" Part — Recovery Beyond Stretching

Stretching is one input. These multiply its effect:

- **Breathwork is the master switch.** Long-exhale breathing (e.g., 4-in / 6-to-8-out) shifts you into parasympathetic recovery. Use it *inside* every stretch.
- **Sleep is the #1 recovery tool.** 7–9 hours. No routine out-trains poor sleep.
- **Hydration + protein** drive tissue repair. Stretching mobilizes, nutrition rebuilds.
- **Move daily, even on rest days.** A 20-min walk flushes more metabolic waste than lying still.
- **Listen to soreness signals.** Light DOMS = green light for gentle flow. Sharp/joint pain = back off and assess.
- **Heat before, not much after.** Warm shower/sauna before deep stretching improves tissue compliance. Cold is better as a separate recovery tool.
- **Contract–relax (PNF) for stubborn ranges:** in a stretch, gently push against resistance 5 sec, then relax and sink deeper. Repeat 2–3×. One of the fastest ways to gain flexibility.

## Methodology & Credits

This routine synthesizes principles from widely respected teachers and systems
in mobility, recovery, and flexibility. Learn directly from the sources — they
go far deeper than any single document:

- **Dr. Kelly Starrett** (*The Ready State* / "Becoming a Supple Leopard") — the couch stretch, joint mechanics, daily mobility practice.
- **Dr. Andreo Spina** (**Functional Range Conditioning / FRC**) — **CARs** (Controlled Articular Rotations) and the idea that *mobility = usable, controlled range*, not just passive flexibility.
- **Pavel Tsatsouline** (*Relax into Stretch*) — tension/relaxation and PNF-style "contract–relax" techniques for fast range gains.
- **Tom Myers** (*Anatomy Trains*) — the body works in connected fascial lines, so we stretch chains (posterior chain, spirals) not just isolated muscles.
- **Ido Portal** & the movement-culture community — CARs, 90/90, and treating mobility as a daily practice.
- **Classical Hatha & Yin Yoga** — the long-hold poses (pigeon, child's pose, butterfly, legs-up-the-wall) and breath-led nervous-system regulation.
- **Dr. Andrew Huberman** (Huberman Lab) — physiological sighs / long-exhale breathing and sleep as primary recovery levers.
- **PNF research (proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation)** — the evidence base behind contract–relax stretching.

## Safety

This is general educational information, not medical advice. If you have an
injury, joint instability, are pregnant, or have any condition affecting your
bones/joints/nerves, check with a physical therapist or physician before
starting. Stop any movement that causes sharp, radiating, or joint pain.
